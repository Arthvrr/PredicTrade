How Linear Regression works

Linear Regression is a statistical method that tries to draw a straight line through the data. It assumes that the future price will follow the same linear trajectory as the past.

While it is excellent for finding the Global Trend (the grey dashed line), it often struggles to adapt to sudden market shocks or complex conditions like volume spikes, because the stock market is highly non-linear.

Probable Range (RMSE): The Root Mean Squared Error measures the average distance between the model's past predictions and the actual prices. We use it to create a confidence bracket around tomorrow's prediction.

How Random Forest works

Random Forest is an advanced ensemble learning algorithm. Instead of trying to draw a single line, it generates hundreds of randomized "Decision Trees" during training.

To predict tomorrow's price, it asks all 100 trees to analyze the current data (Price, Volume, Moving Averages) and then averages their answers. Because it uses branching logic rather than lines, it is incredibly powerful at capturing non-linear dynamics (e.g., "If SMA 5 crosses SMA 10 AND Volume is high, then the stock will pump").

You'll often notice that its Probable Range adjusts more dynamically to recent volatility than the Linear Regression model.
